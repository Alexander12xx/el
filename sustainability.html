Growing responsibly

Fewer inputs, longer life

Water, soil and chemical use are the three things that decide whether flower farming stays viable. This is what we do about each of them.

The short version

Cut flowers are a high-input crop. That is the problem.

Growing export-quality roses means steady water, steady nutrition and constant attention to pests. Done carelessly, that is expensive for the grower and tiring on the soil. We work with farms that are moving in the other direction, because a farm that cannot keep its soil alive cannot keep supplying us.

We are not a certification body and we do not claim to be one. What we do is require a written approach at every farm we buy from, visit through the season rather than at harvest, and pay for the practices that take effort up front.

  • Written terms, not spot payments Growers are paid on agreed terms, which makes investment in soil and irrigation possible.
  • Season-long visits Our agronomists inspect crops during the cycle, so problems are caught early.
  • Honest claims only If a practice is not verified, we do not list it as a feature of our supply.
Three priorities

Where we concentrate

01

Water

Drip irrigation replaces overhead watering on the farms we supply, cutting usage per stem and keeping leaves dry, which in turn reduces fungal pressure.

  • Drip lines under the crop
  • Morning watering windows
  • Mulch to cut evaporation
02

Soil

Crop rotation, composted organic matter and reduced tillage keep structure intact, so less fertiliser is needed to hold the same stem quality.

  • Rotation between crops
  • Composted organic matter
  • Cover between cycles
03

Chemical use

Integrated pest management comes first. Monitoring and biological controls handle most pressure, and sprays are targeted rather than scheduled.

  • Scouting and thresholds
  • Biological controls first
  • Targeted application only
Beyond the farm

Waste on our side of the chain

Most of the environmental cost of flowers happens at the farm, but packing creates waste we can reduce ourselves.

Reusable crates

Field crates travel between the growing house and the pack floor repeatedly rather than being discarded after one trip.

Minimal packaging

Sleeves and ties sized to the order, with recyclable cardboard cartons and no unnecessary void fill.

Offcuts composted

Leaves, stems and rejected foliage go back into the compost stream on the farm instead of to waste.
