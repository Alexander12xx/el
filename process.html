How an order moves

Five stages, each one documented. Select a stage to see what happens between the greenhouse and the carton.

01 · Growing

Care begins at the source.

Varieties are chosen with our growers and matched to soil, altitude and buyer demand before a single stem is planted.

Detail

What happens at each stage

Planting stock is selected against an agreed variety list, then established in the greenhouse or open field. Growers manage nutrition, irrigation and pest pressure on a written schedule, and our agronomists visit through the cycle rather than only at harvest. Colour and firmness targets are set with the buyer in mind from the outset, which is why we can promise consistency later in the season.

Cutting happens in the coolest part of the day so the stems arrive at the pack house with maximum water uptake. Flowers are cut at the bud stage the buyer specified — tight for long-distance programmes, more open for nearby markets. Cut stems go straight into hydration rather than being left in the field, and each lot is labelled at the point of harvest.

Stems are sorted by length, caliper, bud openness, petal count and any blemish or damage. Anything that misses the specification is removed rather than downgraded, and a sample photograph is shared with the buyer before the lot is released to packing. This is the stage that protects the reputation of everything that follows it.

Bunches are built to the agreed stem count, sleeved, labelled and packed into the buyer's carton format with the correct spacing and protective material. Cartons are marked with the lot code so the consignment can be traced back to the field it was cut in, then held in the cool store ready for consolidation.

Lots are consolidated at Nairobi, where the phytosanitary documentation is issued and the consignment is presented for inspection. Air freight is booked against the buyer's delivery window, with cold-chain monitoring through the journey. On arrival the buyer receives the lot codes, packing list and quality record for the shipment.
